HOW TO READ THIS

Useful profiles. Bounded conclusions.

One period, different people

These are repeated cross-sectional surveys of the US civilian, noninstitutionalized population. Age differences may reflect cohort, medication, health, or survival differences. They are not individual aging trajectories or causal effects. Assays and sampling can change between cycles; this dashboard does not estimate cross-cycle trends.

Survey design matters

Means use examination weights, or component-specific fasting weights for glucose, triglycerides and calculated LDL. Standard errors use R’s survey package, strata and primary sampling units. The design is built before selecting age and sex domains. Intervals use a t distribution with domain design degrees of freedom.

Missingness stays visible

Only observed eligible measurements enter each mean. No participant values are imputed by this project and no high values are trimmed. CDC-provided values below detection limits are retained. Missing counts refer to eligible, positive-weight participants; they do not include interview-only participants or zero-weight records. Item nonresponse can still bias estimates.

Exploration, not diagnosis

Adults are aged 20 or older. Age 80+ is an open-ended category, not an age midpoint. Sex categories follow the released RIAGENDR coding. Profiles are not clinical reference ranges, disease prevalence estimates, or measures of biological age. No statistical tests of group differences are performed.
